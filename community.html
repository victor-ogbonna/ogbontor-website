Community

The largest student hardware tech community in South-East Nigeria.

Build nights, study groups, mentors, a component library and project teams. The infrastructure that turns interest into ability.

What membership gets you

Eight things that only work with other people

You can learn to code alone. Hardware needs a bench, a scope, a spare part, and someone who has seen this failure before.

Weekly build nights

Bring what you are working on, or pick up a community build. Benches, tools, and people who already made your mistake.

Study groups

Small groups on the same module. Better than grinding a datasheet alone at 2am.

Mentorship pairing

Paired with someone two steps ahead — near enough to remember being stuck where you are.

Component library

Borrow the sensor, the analyser, the dev board. A missing part should not stop a project.

Demo days

Every cohort ends showing its work to an audience that asks hard questions.

Hackathons

Competitive builds against a brief. The quickest way to find out what you know.

Project teams

Multi-month teams on the systems under Projects, with roles and a real deadline.

Internships & placement

Introductions to partner companies, and support taking a capstone to product.

What the week looks like

Come for one evening and see

One open lab evening a week, study groups around whichever modules are running, and project teams on their own schedule.

During the three-week bootcamp the pace changes — cohorts meet most days.

Dates and venue are announced in the community WhatsApp group. Build-night times, cohort start dates and any change of venue all go out there first — join the group to get them.

How we behave

Code of conduct

Beginners have to be safe here. The rules are short and we enforce them.

  • No condescension. Everyone was a beginner recently. Answer the question that was asked.
  • Credit honestly. Name who did the work, cite the reference, disclose what was borrowed.
  • Share what you learn. If you solved it, write it down where the next person will find it.
  • Respect the equipment. Shared tools and borrowed parts come back, on time and working.
  • Zero tolerance for harassment. On any grounds, in the lab or the channel.
Questions

Things people ask before joining

Do I need any electronics experience to join?
No. The foundational tier assumes nothing — it starts at what current and voltage are and why a resistor matters. Most members arrive having never soldered anything.
Do I have to be a student at UNN?
No — and you do not have to be in Nigeria at all. The lab is at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, and that is where the benches, build nights and bootcamps are, but membership is open to anyone, anywhere in the world. If you can get to Nsukka you can be a full member with lab access; if you cannot, you can still take part in the community channel, the study groups and remote project teams.

The conference and the free three-week bootcamp are specific to UNN Nsukka for this first run. Other South-East universities are next — at least one per state.
Does it cost anything?
Associate and Builder membership are free, and so are bootcamp seats. The cost barrier is the thing we exist to remove. Funded capstones are handled case by case.
What course do I need to be studying?
Any. Our members come from electrical engineering, computer science, mechanical, physics, and from outside the sciences entirely. Hardware needs people who can design enclosures and talk to users as much as it needs firmware engineers.
Do I need my own laptop or components?
It helps, but no. The component library lends boards, sensors and tools, and lab machines are open during lab hours.
How much time does it take?
A build night is one evening a week. The bootcamp runs three weeks and is far more intense. Project teams set their own pace.
Can my company partner or sponsor?
Yes — equipment, component budgets, internship places and project sponsorship are all useful to us. Write to victorogbonna313@gmail.com and say what you have in mind.
What happens after the capstone?
The strongest go into incubation — IP, a pitch, pilot users, company formation. Both portfolio companies, Joint-Agent and CNG-Protect, started this way.
