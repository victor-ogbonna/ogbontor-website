About us

We are building the engineers who will build Nigeria's hardware.

Ogbontor Engineering Enterprise is a hardware technology enterprise and student community based at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka. We train, we prototype, and we turn what works into products.

The problem

Africa consumes technology it cannot repair

Nigeria, and indeed much of the African continent, faces a significant setback in technological development. Almost everything electronic in daily use here was designed somewhere else, for someone else, and arrives as a sealed box.

When it fails, it is replaced rather than fixed. When it doesn't suit local conditions — unstable mains, dust, heat, intermittent connectivity — it is tolerated rather than redesigned. And the engineering capacity that would let us do otherwise never gets built, because there is nowhere to learn it properly and nothing to practise on.

That is a training problem before it is an industrial one. It is the one we decided to work on.

Our position

The capacity is already here

Ogbontor is dedicated to fostering a new era of Nigerian technological innovation. We believe Nigeria possesses the inherent capacity to address its own technological challenges, rather than depending on solutions designed for other contexts.

What has been missing is not talent. It is a bench, a curriculum that goes deep enough to matter, and a room full of people working on the same problem at the same time. We assembled those three things in Nsukka, and the result is now the largest student hardware tech community in South-East Nigeria.

  • Founded and operating from the University of Nigeria, Nsukka
  • Registered in Nigeria
  • Thirteen hardware systems designed, built or under active development
  • Membership open to students across South-East Nigeria, free of charge
How we work

Four things we refuse to compromise on

Hands on the hardware

Nobody learns embedded systems from a slide deck. Every module ends with something on a bench that either works or doesn't.

Nobody builds alone

Seniors teach juniors. The person who solved your bug last month is in the room. That loop is the whole community.

Built for here

Designed for Nigerian power, Nigerian roads, Nigerian budgets and Nigerian users — not adapted from a product made for somewhere else.

Rigour, not theatre

Datasheets get read. Designs get reviewed. Claims get measured. An impressive demo that cannot be reproduced is not an achievement.

Long-term objectives

What we are actually aiming at

These are deliberately larger than a student club should attempt. Stating them plainly is how we keep the curriculum honest about how deep it has to go.

Indigenous consumer electronics

Smartphones, televisions and household electronics designed and assembled in Nigeria — which is why the curriculum runs all the way to CPU and GPU architecture.

Vehicles, aircraft and submersibles

Transport engineered for African terrain, climate and infrastructure rather than imported and then apologised for.

Smart systems that fit the culture

Connected systems built around how Nigerian homes, markets, clinics and farms actually operate.

Illustrative concepts — these show the direction of the roadmap, not facilities currently in operation.
